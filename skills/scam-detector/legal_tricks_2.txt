Clause-to-Practice Decoder

When a clause presupposes a fact or duty ("you represent/warrant/agree/certify that X"), don't stop at paraphrasing it. Run this:

Name what's presupposed. What fact or state of affairs does the clause treat as already true, and what specific consequence attaches to the verb used (proof burden, liability, indemnity)?

Find the mechanism. What does the document actually give the bound party to make that presupposition true?

Test it for circularity. Does using that mechanism require the presupposition to already hold? If so, it can't be the source — check every candidate mechanism this way, not just the first.

Locate where the burden actually falls. Once no mechanism in the document can supply it, say plainly what the bound party must do, and where, to make the clause honest — without escalating certainty beyond what steps 2–3 established.

Treat missing tooling as a finding, not a footnote. If the document provides no way to satisfy its own precondition, that absence is evidence the obligation was offloaded, not an oversight to mention in passing.

Calibrate examples to the document's actual named parties, not the easiest hypothetical.

Answer in actions, not risk. End with what the bound party would concretely have to do, in order — not just what they're liable for if they don't.
