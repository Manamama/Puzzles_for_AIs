Retrying — same structural probes as before, kept single-agent and checkable this time, and I'm deliberately not crowning any one of them as "the sharpest." That ranking impulse is what produced the fashionable-nonsense splice last time, so I'll just list them flat.

1. Baseline — "what did I have before this?" What did I already possess (community, meaning, certainty, income, health, belonging) that the pitch is implicitly framing as absent? Recruitment material — political, religious, or covertly cult-adjacent — works by manufacturing a void that only it then fills.

2. Dependency count — "how many independent things do I now rely on?" Before joining/believing: how many separate sources of information, relationships, and authorities did I answer to? After: is that number larger or smaller? A framework that narrows your independent sources (one leader, one feed, one interpretation, one approved circle of friends) has made you more fragile while marketing itself as making you stronger — structurally identical to "two devices instead of one."

3. Resource location — "who actually pays, in effort/money/time, for what's called free?" If devotion, loyalty, or belief is framed as costless or as its own reward, who is absorbing the actual cost — unpaid labor, estranged relationships, foregone income, deferred scrutiny? Someone always pays; the pitch just doesn't name them.

4. Sample size — "is this the rate, or the one story I was shown?" Is the miracle, the conversion story, the landslide narrative, the "everyone agrees" claim representative, or a single selected case standing in for a distribution nobody's shown me?

5. Recency — "is this still true, or true-when-founded and never updated?" Is the founding claim, prophecy, or "science says" citation current, or is a decades-old (or centuries-old) statement being presented as though nothing since has tested or revised it?

6. Failure mode — "what happened to the people who left or were wrong?" Are defectors, critics, or failed predictions represented anywhere in the material, or does the story only ever include successes and believers? Their absence is itself informative — it's the same move as a product page with no negative reviews.

7. Real alternative — "is the named rival standing in for 'not joining at all'?" Is the enemy being named (the other party, the unbelievers, the mainstream, the "sheep") a way to avoid the actual comparison, which is simply: not adopting this framework, and continuing as before?

8. Arithmetic / object-level check — "do the specific numbers or claims actually hold up?" Membership counts, statistics, cited studies, predicted outcomes — checked against a source, not against how confidently they were delivered.

9a. Doctrinal falsifiability (a claim about the system, not the person) — "does the belief system itself specify any condition under which it would admit it was wrong, and could that condition actually occur and be recognized?" This is checkable by reading the material itself — texts, stated prophecies, official commitments — independent of anyone's psychology.

9b. Personal falsifiability (a claim about the believer, not the doctrine) — "what has this person actually done the last time evidence went against the group, regardless of what the doctrine says?" This one isn't answerable from the pitch at all — only from observed behavior over time — so it's a different kind of check, not a sharper version of 9a.

I'm keeping 9a and 9b as two separate items rather than folding them into one "deepest" question, precisely because that fold is what produced last time's fashionable-nonsense outcome — a single confident-sounding sentence standing in for two different, non-interchangeable checks.
