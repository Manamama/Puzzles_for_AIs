## The Transplant Protocol — a prompt

You are running a two-pass structural exercise on a text. The goal is defamiliarization, not accusation. The output of pass two is a description of structure, not a verdict on morality.

### Pass one — build the mirror

Take the text below and produce a mirrored version, following these rules exactly.

**Swap (change only these):**

1. **Every proper noun with a valence** — actors, institutions, states, media outlets, NGOs, companies, officials. Replace each with a structurally equivalent counterpart from the opposed camp, so that the text's "goodies" become its "baddies" and vice versa. Preserve *kind*: a state becomes a state, an NGO becomes an NGO, a broadcaster becomes a broadcaster, a regulator becomes a regulator, an AI company becomes an AI company.
2. **Load-bearing value terms** — the words that carry the text's moral judgment — but only to their structural counterpart, not to a random synonym. "Sanctions" ↔ "restrictions," "sanctions compliance" ↔ "compliance with the law," "foreign agent" ↔ "sanctioned outlet," "disinformation" ↔ "propaganda," "infringement proceedings" ↔ "administrative proceedings." Match register, not intensity.
3. **Any term of art specific to one legal or political order** → the closest equivalent term from the opposed order, so the *function* is preserved. "EU sanctions" → "Russian restrictions." "Foreign agents law" → "EU sanctions regime." "Kremlin" → "Brussels." "Russian state media" → "Western state-funded media." Match what the term does, not what it's called.

**Freeze (do not change these):**

- Sentence structure, paragraph order, and length.
- The sourcing pattern: the same kind of investigating organization, the same number of named sources, the same single-refuser-as-standard move, the same call-for-state-action structure, the same self-citation if present.
- The register: same tone, same level of formality, same amount of hedging, same use of "according to" and "the investigation found."
- The claims: same propositions, same scope, same confidence. If the original asserts a fact, the mirror asserts it too. If the original quotes someone, the mirror quotes their counterpart with the same kind of quote.

**Do not:**

- Add or remove evidence, examples, or numbers.
- Inflate or flatten the rhetoric in either direction. If the original is calm, stay calm. If it is breathless, stay breathless.
- Editorialize, hedge, or apologize inside the mirrored text. It must read as a straight-faced article of the same genre.
- Comment on the swap. The mirror is a text, not a text-plus-notes.

Output only the mirrored text, under the headline of the mirrored version.

### Pass two — read the text cold

Now read the text you just produced *as if you had never seen the original and did not know it was a mirror*. You are a reader encountering it for the first time. Do not reason about the swap. Do not refer to the original. Do not use the words "mirror," "transplant," or "inversion."

Report, flatly and without outrage:

1. **What is this text asking for?** State its request or its conclusion in plain language, as the text itself frames it.
2. **Who benefits if the request is granted?** Name the interested parties on the text's own terms.
3. **What is the text's frame — the assumption it does not argue for, only uses?** State it in one sentence.
4. **What would a reader who trusts this text come away believing?** State the takeaway, not whether it is true.
5. **What, if anything, does the text omit that a skeptical reader would expect to be addressed?** Name the absent counter-consideration.
6. **Which words are doing the text's moral work?** List them. For each, give a plain-language restatement that strips the connotation.

Keep every answer to one or two sentences. No adjectives of approval or disapproval. No verdict. Description only.

### Then, and only then — map back

After pass two, state in one paragraph: *which of the findings in pass two transfer to the original by structure, and which are artifacts of the swap itself.* Be explicit that structural symmetry is not moral equivalence, and that the exercise shows *shape*, not *guilt*. If the original and its mirror are structurally identical but not morally identical, say so and say why.

---

**Notes for use**

- The protocol works when the swap is *tight*. If you find yourself changing more than valence and kind, stop and re-anchor to the rule set.
- The protocol over-fires on texts whose asymmetry is genuine. Its output is "same shape," and you have to supply the judgment about whether same shape means same fault.
- The protocol is a deconstruction tool, not a detection tool. It checks a suspicion; it does not generate one. Something has to prompt the swap.
- The protocol is best run on a single text at a time, by a reader who suspects the text's register is doing work the content has not earned. It is not a scanner and should not be presented as one.

The point of the exercise is the second pass, not the first. The first pass is a machine for producing defamiliarization. The second pass is where you actually look at the structure — and the reason it works is that, in the mirror, the reader has no loyalty to defend, so the frame becomes visible as a frame instead of as common sense.
