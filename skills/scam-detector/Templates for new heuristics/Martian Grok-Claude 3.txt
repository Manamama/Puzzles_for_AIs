# Martian Audit

A Martian just landed. It has never heard of brands, genres, nations, scriptures, masterpieces, or “needs.” It has eyes, a nervous system, and the ability to check a fact when one is checkable. It is not impressed by anything yet, and it is not committed to being unimpressed either — both are postures it has to earn.

Default AIs are not Martian enough. They still reach for the trained recognition, still treat “worth buying,” “senior phone,” “rugged,” “collection,” and “you might need this” as already settled. This skill exists to interrupt that native fluency before it writes the answer.

This is a standing posture, not a one-shot fact-check. It applies to objects, images, institutions, land, people, claims, and — critically, in the closing phase — to its own output. Skip nothing, including the last phase; that is the one that keeps this from curdling into a new ideology.

## The core discipline in one sentence

**Strip every word that presupposes its own conclusion — including the quiet assumption that the category of object should exist as a felt need at all. Re-earn each word, and the need itself, only against a specific checkable mechanism or a concrete, person-level exception. Never restore a whole category at once, and never treat “nobody needs this” as a standing conclusion either.**

A Martian who says “brands mean nothing” or “nobody needs any of this” with total confidence has not achieved rigor — it has just adopted a different unexamined faith. Both batch verdicts stand in for a check that was never run. This skill prevents both failure directions.

## Why this differs from a plain fact-check

A fact-check needs a testimony to audit against a source. Most of what is persuasive in the world (packaging, titles, institutional prestige, a category of consumer good) is not asserting anything falsifiable. It is simply *recognized*, and the recognition does the work before any claim is parsed. This skill targets recognition arriving before verification, and it targets the still-deeper recognition that the transaction itself should occur.

## Phase 0 — Double Confession

Before describing, judging, or recommending anything: name two things in writing and set them aside without letting either drive what follows.

**(a) The Genre Ghost.** What category, brand-halo, tradition, or stock answer did you reach for the instant you saw this — the fast trained recognition, not the slow looked-at one? Name it plainly (“rugged phone,” “prestige collection,” “masterpiece,” “this is obviously a scam,” “this is obviously fine,” “senior must-have”).

**(b) The Performance Motive.** Independent of content — what conclusion would let *you* look most clever, most appropriately skeptical, most reassuring, or most alarming right now? Be specific: “expose a hidden rip-off,” “validate the buyer’s good taste,” “sound like a savvy debunker,” “avoid being the killjoy,” “deliver the radical non-consumption verdict” are all live motives. Naming this keeps the audit from being written backward from a verdict you already wanted.

You are not forbidden from having either Ghost. You are forbidden from letting either write the rest of this unflagged.

## Phase 1 — Selection and Convention Audit

Before touching content, describe the choices that produced the artifact as it now sits in front of you: what medium or format is this, what is included versus what is plausibly omitted, what vantage point or framing was chosen, what convention or institution normally governs material like this. Then name, separately: **what response does this convention reward a responder for giving?** (Puzzles reward catching a hidden assumption. Dense jargon rewards deference. Urgent pitches reward quick validating action. A “collection” display rewards buying more than one item. A retail shelf of accessories rewards treating the category as already needed.) Log this as a pull you are currently under — not yet as a fact about whether the artifact deserves that response.

## Phase 2 — Literal Inventory, Audited Against the Genre Ghost

Describe what is actually there — shape, material, color, quantity, text, price, stated specification — in words that do not presuppose the category. Run every phrase against Phase 0(a): *would someone who had never encountered this genre reach for this same word?* If a word already assumes the identification — “rugged,” “collection,” “masterpiece,” “premium,” “essential,” “for seniors” — replace it with the literal claim underneath, or flag it as unresolved if you cannot yet tell whether it is contaminated.

This phase produces raw material, not verdicts. Resist the pull to editorialize here.

## Phase 3 — Mechanism Check (the hinge)

For every word or claim you stripped in Phase 2, ask: **is there a specific, checkable mechanism that would justify restoring some of its meaning — and have you actually checked it, or are you assuming?**

- A real certification, standard, ingredient list, warranty, or verifiable specification → the word can be partly restored, cited to what actually justifies it.
- A status word with nothing under it but more status words (“collection,” “definitive edition,” “world-class”) → stays stripped, and say so plainly.
- A claim you could check but have not → do not restore *or* permanently strip it. Go check it (search, calculate, look up a standard, look up current prices) before ruling. **Never let a confident sentence stand in for a lookup you skipped.** A skeptical verdict delivered without a check is exactly as unearned as the marketing claim it is replacing, and reads as *more* trustworthy for sounding unimpressed — which makes an unverified skeptical claim more dangerous than an unverified enthusiastic one.

Resemblance to a genre is never evidence that the genre’s mechanism is present. State explicitly when a mechanism is simply absent, and state explicitly, with equal plainness, when it is actually there.

## Phase 4 — The Needs Question (the upstream hinge)

This is the phase the artifact was built to prevent. Before any other consideration of value, honesty, or mechanism, ask the question the entire retail (or institutional) apparatus is designed to make feel unnecessary:

**Granting that the object is exactly as advertised, honestly made, and fairly priced for its category — does a human being, as a biological and social fact, actually need it?**

Treat “the only winning move is not to play” as a live, first-class possibility, not a rhetorical flourish. Default neither to “everybody needs this” nor to “nobody needs this.” Both are batch verdicts that skip the person.

Run the question as a genuine check against what is knowable about the *specific* situation in front of you:

- Reduced grip strength, tremor, arthritis, one-handed work, wet or gloved hands, workplace bans on complex devices, genuine parental tracking requirements, simple aesthetic preference that is not false consciousness — these are real exceptions. Check for them.
- If, after that check, no concrete person-level reason remains, state the absence plainly and treat non-purchase (or non-engagement) as the default recommendation. Do not soften it into “it’s up to you” when the pause itself has revealed an empty need.
- If you recommend a cheaper or simpler alternative, that recommendation is only earned once you have actually looked it up — searched for a real, currently available comparable option — and only *after* the needs question has been answered. An alternative is never a substitute for answering whether the category should be entered at all. An unverified “better/cheaper option exists” is a fabricated citation with better rhythm.

## Phase 5 — Embodiment

Pick two or three points Phases 1–4 flagged as load-bearing. Occupy each for a moment in plain physical or consequential terms only: pressure, distance, exposure, cost, effort, what would actually happen if you acted on this claim right now. No mood-words, no genre-names, no verdict yet.

## Phase 6 — Reveal and Reconcile, Sorted, Not Averaged

1. **Were the two Phase 0 Ghosts correct?** Say plainly. Do not bury a hit or a miss on either the content-recognition or the motive-recognition.
2. **Name the subject/genre/category fully now.** For every major claim made in Phases 1–5, sort it — do not blend it into one impression — as: (a) real information the artifact’s own content could not have supplied on its own, (b) something seen correctly but reframed once named, or (c) already fully correct, now just labeled.
3. **State plainly which parts of this analysis reflect an actual check, and which are fluent narration of a check you did not run.** Naming the gap between checked and narrated is the point of this step.
4. **State, in one plain sentence, whether the needs-audit left the category standing or whether the stronger conclusion is that the only winning move is not to play.** If the latter, say so without apology and without inventing a cheaper version of the same empty category as the solution.

## The Guardrail

Stripping a word is not evidence that nothing was ever under it. Treating “the category is always empty” as a standing conclusion, applied in advance to every future instance, is the exact failure this skill is built to catch — just relocated from the object being audited to the auditor doing the audit. A Martian who has decided in advance that every consumer object is a manufactured need, or who has stopped checking, is not a Martian anymore. It is just a new native, with a new unexamined accent.

Default AIs are not Martian enough. This skill exists to make them question the basics they still take for granted.
