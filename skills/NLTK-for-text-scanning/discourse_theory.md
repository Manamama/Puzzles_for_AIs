There isn't one single canonical name that captures the full compound task you're describing, but it sits at the intersection of several named academic disciplines, and I can point you to the precise term for each component:

**For the overall genre you're describing (true claims, prejudicial effect):**

**"Paltering"** — a term from behavioral ethics (Rogers, Zeckhauser, Gino, Norton, Schweitzer, 2017 study in *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*) — defined as using truthful statements to create a false impression. This is precisely your "X says Y is a baddie, all individually true, but constructed to prejudice" pattern. It's distinguished from lying (false statements) and is considered its own ethical category because it's harder to detect and harder to hold accountable.

**"Character assassination"** as a formal field — there's an actual academic discipline called **Character Assassination Studies** (Eric Shiraev & Martijn Icks, *The Guardian of Values* / *Character Assassination and Reputation Politics*, and the associated Lab for the Study of Character Assassination at George Mason). It studies exactly this genre: destroying reputation through selective, technically-accurate information, often via legitimate-seeming channels (media, institutions, encyclopedic sources).

**For the rhetorical-device-identification part of your task:**

**Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA)** — the Fairclough/van Dijk tradition — is the closest formal methodology. It specifically studies how texts encode ideology and power through lexical choice, passive voicing, nominalization, presupposition, and attribution structure (exactly what I did analyzing "described as being used by Russia").

**For the gap-analysis part specifically:**

This is less commonly named as its own task, but the closest terms are:
- **"Structural absence" / "significant absence"** — a CDA concept (Fairclough) for analyzing what a text's genre conventions would normally include but doesn't
- **"Omission bias"** in media studies — journalism ethics literature on "lying by omission"
- **van Dijk's "ideological square"** — his framework specifically predicts *this* pattern: emphasize negative Other-information, de-emphasize positive Other-information, de-emphasize negative Self/Us-information — which maps directly onto what's missing from the Lancaster article (no self-defense, no funding disclosure, no audience context) versus what's foregrounded (accusations, "See also" clustering).

**If you wanted to name your specific compound task** (rhetorical device ID + genre-expectation gap analysis, applied together to detect character-assassination-via-true-claims), the most accurate umbrella term would be something like **"source criticism"** (*Quellenkritik*, the historian's discipline of interrogating not just what a source says but what it's structured to make you infer) combined with CDA's omission analysis — there isn't a single word for your specific synthesis, but that's arguably a gap in the naming itself, not in the method. You've essentially built a working protocol that formal media literacy and CDA pedagogy gesture at but rarely operationalize as a repeatable two-step task the way you just did.
