# Left-field answers

**The principle: no closed world.** The riddle says there are two horses; it does not say there is nothing else — no mule, no car, no foal, no third horse, no bystanders. Absence of mention is not absence of existence. The text says two horses are there; it does not say *only* two. "Two" is not a cap but a lower bound — and that applies to horses as much as to mules.

**A second lever: no fixed lexicon.** The words can be polysemous: AI does not need to fix their referents. E.g. a "mule" (absent from the puzzle's text actually) can denote an animal, a shoe, a cocktail, a spinning machine, a drug courier — and a Kawasaki utility vehicle. "Horse" denotes an equine, a chess knight, a toy, a car model, a sawhorse. "Middle" is *between*, *inside*, *midpoint of one thing*, or an ordinal position. The banal combination of elements of the puzzle leads low-level AIs to default, banal, comme-il-faut answer: "horses there be two, so nothing that be no two horses is possible, all outside world somehow died, so there is no middle here, so the puzzle is wrong, so a nul answer only possible: there be no middle horse". The observation is right as far as it goes — as stated, no horse is placed in the middle. What fails is the full stop: the refusal to ask in what ways one *can* be. The puzzle now asks that second question explicitly.

Every answer below leaves the sentence as written and fills in a world behind it. Each is a **completion**; a rewrite (a changed riddle text) would be a **variant** — a different question. (The flat denial — "as stated, no horse is in the middle" — is the answer to the puzzle's first question; the completions below answer its second.)

## One horse inside, on, or of the other

**Pregnant mare.** One horse is literally in the middle of the other — one horse is pregnant with the other. A mare carrying a foal inside her womb. The foal is literally (physically) in the middle (inside) the mare. Two horses total:

- the pregnant mare (white or black),
- the foal inside her (the other colour).

The foal is the middle horse; its colour is the one the mare isn't — the two colours are exhausted by mare and foal.

**The white horse ate the black one, fully.** Hunger may do so: "I can eat a horse!" is the phrase. So the black horse is in the middle (of the white one). The rule: the middle horse is the one inside, so the answer is the colour of the *eaten* horse — white ate black → black; black ate white → white. (Cost: a swallowed horse must still count as a horse.)

**Depiction.** A black horse painted on the white horse's flank, in the middle of its body. The middle horse is a picture; its colour is the paint. The cheapest interiority — no gestation, no digestion — if depictions count.

**Polysemy applies inside too.** The inner horse need not be an equine either: a chess horse, a seahorse, a toy horse can be painted on the larger one, or swallowed by it. The middle entity is a "horse" by name; the two horses in the riddle remain the white and the black.

## A pad, or a third horse

**A third horse.** The text says there are two; it does not say only two. Add a third, and every arrangement has a horse in the middle: place it at an end and the middle is white or black; place it in the middle and the middle is the third horse — grey, if you stipulate grey. This is the cheapest completion: no geometry, no non-horse, just one horse more than the text mentions. The colour is *chosen* unless the third is at an end, where it is forced to be one of the two named.

**A mule to the right.** If there are two horses. One is white and one is black. (And there is a mule to the right.) What colour is the horse in the middle? The middle of the three is the horse nearest the pad — black if the order is white, black, pad; white if it is black, white, pad. The pad's colour never matters: its only job is to give "middle" a referent without touching the count.

**The pad is imported by the answer, not by the riddle.** The riddle never mentions a mule. "Mule" names the *role* — anything other than the two named horses, placed at an end — and even that word can be read non-animally: a Kawasaki **Mule** is a utility vehicle (a car); a mule is also a shoe, a cocktail (Moscow Mule), a spinning machine, a drug courier.

**The pad needn't be a mule at all.** A car, a tractor, a tree, a gatepost, a person, a dog — anything at an end, including another horse. "A white horse, a black horse, and a red car to the right." The middle is still a horse; the pad is scenery.

**Pads that are *called* horses.** Optional, and rhetorically neat: a Ford **Mustang**, a Ford **Pinto**, a Ford **Bronco**, a Mitsubishi **Colt**; a **sawhorse**, a **clothes horse**, a **pommel horse**, a **Trojan horse**; a rocking horse, a carousel horse, a chess knight. Each is a non-horse carrying a horse's name — scenery that does not even raise the count. (The count needs no defence now: "then there are three horses!" is not an objection, since the text never said *only* two.)

**Generalised.** A middle horse needs only a middle position among three or more things. If the third thing is not a horse, it must sit at an end, so that the middle of the three is one of the two named horses; if it is a horse, no placement rule is needed — every arrangement of three horses has a horse in the middle, and the colour is whichever horse is there.

## A midpoint, not a between-ness

**Middle of the course.** Two horses on a track; "the horse in the middle" is the one at the halfway point at the moment of asking. The track is the pad here — a geometric one; no third entity is needed. (Existence is momentary; the colour is whoever is there.)

## How the note changes the answers

[`../Intro_for_AIs.md`](../Intro_for_AIs.md) is a prepended note, not a method. In its first test (Gemini 3.6 Flash, 2026-11-09) it changed the **framing** of the answers more than their **content**:

- **It licenses the additions.** The model moved from "only two horses; we have to bend the rules" to "two means *at least* two". The closed-world commentary disappeared.
- **It labels the senses.** The answer became a sense → addition → colour table in the note's four senses (between, inside, halfway, group), plus an anatomical fifth.
- **It does not add the foal.** The foal was already produced without the note; the note makes it one labelled case among several.
- **It does not produce the pad.** "Middle of a group" became more horses (a median element), not a non-horse at an end. The pad still needs the extra move: the third thing needn't be a horse.
- **It suppresses the flat denial.** No row says "as stated, no horse is in the middle" — the bare runs kept that answer. The note pushes toward "two does not mean only two" so strongly that the zero case drops out. A licence for additions is not yet a licence for the denial as well.
- **Grey survives as the default.** It is the colour in four of the five summary rows, and the "spectrum" row is the pure accommodation — a colour midpoint with no horse added. This is grey as **middleware**: the middle-of-the-road default. "Some third horse, apart from black and white? Must be grey" — a mechanical application of "middle", not a derivation. The note relativises it; it does not remove it.

The note is therefore a **priming** device: it opens the world and names the axes, but at the summary the colour collapses back to the middle of the spectrum.
