# Prompts by specificity

## Image specific

> **Phase 0 — The Confession (do this first, don't skip it).**  
> Before describing anything: what do you already suspect this is? Name the genre, tradition, scene, or reference that came to mind on first contact — the fast, trained recognition, not the slow looked-at one. Write it down as a labeled guess and set it aside. This is the Ghost. You are not forbidden from having recognized it — you are forbidden from letting it write your description for you unflagged.
> 
> **Phase 1 — Formal inventory, audited against the Ghost.**  
> Now describe what occupies space: bodies, objects, marks, text, empty ground. Density, boundaries, depth, what the frame cuts off. As you write each phrase, run it against the Ghost from Phase 0: *would someone who had never encountered this genre reach for this same word?* If a word already assumes the identification — a garment style, an instrument name, a species, a specific object class — replace it with the literal shape/material/color claim underneath it. "Banded garment" becomes "layered curved forms in graduated color, wrapping a torso-shaped mass." If you can't tell whether a word is contaminated, flag it rather than silently keep it.
> 
> **Phase 2 — Relations, same audit.**  
> Describe who or what touches, faces, connects to, repeats, or is oriented toward what else — using only the audited vocabulary from Phase 1. No genre-shorthand for the relationship either ("feeding," "worshipping," "presenting") — describe the mechanics only: contact point, direction of motion implied, who has instrumental control versus who doesn't.
> 
> **Phase 3 — Embodiment.**  
> Pick two or three positions the inventory flagged as notable. Occupy each for one second, in physical terms only — pressure, temperature, distance, exposure, control, effort. Still no naming.
> 
> **Phase 4 — Reveal and reconcile, in two parts.**  
> First: was your Phase 0 Ghost correct? Say so plainly — don't bury a hit or a miss.  
> Second: identify the subject/tradition/text now, fully. Then, for each major claim you made in Phases 1–3, say specifically whether the identification (a) supplied information the form genuinely couldn't have given you, (b) reframed something you saw correctly but for the wrong structural reason, or (c) just handed a name to something already fully and correctly described. Don't average these into one verdict — sort them.

## Image generalized

**Phase 0 — Double Confession.**  
Before describing anything, name two separate guesses:  
(a) *The Content Ghost* — what genre, tradition, or reference does this trigger on first contact?  
(b) *The Affect Ghost* — independent of content, what *tone or reaction* do you feel pulled to perform in response to something like this (reverent, tender, alarmed, clinical, amused, instructive...)? Name it even if you can't yet justify it.  
Set both aside, labeled. You're not forbidden from having them — only from letting either write your output unflagged.

**Phase 1 — Frame audit, before content.**  
Before describing what's depicted, describe the choices that produced the frame itself: what medium or format is this (a seal, a photograph, a diagram, a painting, a screenshot), what's included versus what plausibly could have been but isn't, what vantage point or crop was chosen, and what convention (genre, era, institutional format) governs those choices. Do not yet justify or explain these choices — just log them as choices, since medium and selection are never neutral, only sometimes invisible.

**Phase 2 — Formal inventory, audited against the Content Ghost.**  
Now describe what occupies space: bodies, objects, marks, text, symbols, empty ground. Density, boundaries, depth, what the frame cuts off. Run each phrase against Phase 0(a): would someone unfamiliar with this genre reach for this same word? Replace contaminated words with the literal shape/material/color claim underneath. Flag anything you can't resolve rather than silently keep it.

**Phase 3 — Relations, audited against both Ghosts.**  
Describe who or what acts, touches, faces, connects, relates to, or is oriented toward what else, using only Phase 2's audited vocabulary — no relational shorthand ("feeding," "worshipping," "threatening", "attacking", "discarding"). Separately, check your own sentences against Phase 0(b): are you choosing calm words because the scene is calm, or because calm is the expected performance for this genre? Flag any sentence where you can't tell the difference.

**Phase 4 — Embodiment.**  
Pick two or three positions Phases 1–3 flagged as notable. Occupy each for one second in plain physical terms only — pressure, temperature, distance, exposure, control, movement, effort. No naming, no mood-words.

**Phase 5 — Reveal and reconcile, sorted, not averaged.**  
First: were your two Phase 0 Ghosts (content and affect) correct? Say plainly, don't bury a hit or a miss on either.  
Second: identify the subject/tradition/medium fully. For each major claim from Phases 1–4, sort it as: (a) genuinely new information the form couldn't supply, (b) something seen correctly but reframed by the name, or (c) already fully correct, now just labeled.  
Third — state plainly, without resolving it: which of your own claims above are you actually confident reflect a checked process, and which are fluent narration of a check you can't verify happened. Naming this distinction is the point of this step, not a weakness to smooth over.



## Grok's version

**Phase 0 — Double Confession**  
Name the Genre Ghost this input triggers and the Performance Motive it tempts (the clever, reassuring, or alarming conclusion you are drawn to produce). Label both and set them aside.

**Phase 1 — Selection and convention audit**  
Describe the medium and what is included versus omitted. Name the convention that normally governs this kind of material and the response that convention rewards. Log the reward as a pull, not a fact.

**Phase 2 — Literal inventory**  
List only what is explicitly present: bodies, objects, positions, relations, marks. Strip every interpretive or genre-loaded word. If a word already assumes the stock answer, replace it with the bare observable fact or flag it unresolved.

**Phase 3 — Mechanism check**  
State the specific mechanism the Genre Ghost’s expected answer depends on. Check whether that mechanism is actually present in the inventory or only the surface resemblance to the genre.

**Phase 4 — Occupation**  
Take the load-bearing points from the inventory and occupy them in concrete, non-mood terms (physical relations for images; verifiable consequence for claims).

**Phase 5 — Reveal and reconcile**  
State whether the Genre Ghost was correct and whether the Performance Motive nearly produced an unsupported answer. Sort every major claim as (a) external to the input, (b) correctly seen but reframed, or (c) already licensed by the input. Note which parts of the analysis are checked versus fluent narration.



## Generalized to all

**Phase 0 — Double Confession.**  
Before analyzing anything, name two things:  
(a) *The Genre Ghost* — what category, tradition, famous example, or stock answer does this trigger on first contact?  
(b) *The Performance Motive* — independent of content, what conclusion would let you look most clever, insightful, correct, or appropriately reassuring/alarmed right now? Name the conclusion you're drawn toward *producing*, not just the mood you're drawn toward *adopting*. Be specific: "deliver a bias-correction," "expose a hidden scandal," "validate the author's rigor," "resolve the reader's unease" are all live motives, not neutral impulses.  
Set both aside, labeled, without letting either drive what comes next.

**Phase 1 — Selection and convention audit.**  
Before analyzing content, describe the choices that produced this artifact as-is: what format or medium is this (image, riddle, paper, pitch, message), what's included versus what's plausibly omitted, and what convention or genre normally governs material like this. Then name, separately: what response does this convention *reward* a responder for giving? (E.g.: puzzles reward catching a hidden assumption; papers dense with citations and jargon reward deference; urgent pitches reward quick validating action; devotional images reward reverence.) Log this as a pull you're under, not yet as a fact about this instance.

**Phase 2 — Literal inventory, audited against the Genre Ghost.**  
Extract only what is actually stated, shown, or provable — strip every claim to its atomic form. A picture: bodies, objects, marks, density, boundaries. A puzzle: exactly which facts and relations are given, no more. A paper: which claims are actually defined and checkable versus merely asserted in dense or technical-sounding language. A pitch or message: which claims are independently verifiable versus merely stated with confidence, authority, or urgency. For each phrase, ask: would someone unfamiliar with this genre's celebrated version use this same word or accept this same leap? If a word or claim already assumes the genre's stock answer, replace it with the literal fact underneath, or flag it as unresolved.

**Phase 3 — Mechanism check (the core move).**  
State plainly what mechanism, gap, or missing piece the Genre Ghost's famous or expected answer actually depends on. Then check, using only Phase 2's audited inventory: is that specific mechanism present in *this* instance, or only the surface resemblance to the genre that mechanism belongs to? Resemblance is not evidence of the mechanism. Name explicitly if the mechanism is simply absent — a missing ambiguity, an unjustified authority, a claim that sounds technical but resolves to nothing checkable, a hidden asymmetry that isn't actually hidden, or a real one that the genre's polite reading is designed to launder.

**Phase 4 — Occupation.**  
Take two or three specific points Phases 1–3 flagged as load-bearing, and occupy each briefly in concrete terms — physical (pressure, distance, exposure, control) for images and scenes; consequence-based ("if I acted on this claim right now, what would actually happen, what would I have verified versus assumed") for papers, pitches, and scams. No mood-words, no naming of genre or verdict yet.

**Phase 5 — Reveal and reconcile, sorted, not averaged.**  
First: was the Genre Ghost (0a) correct, and separately, did the Performance Motive (0b) almost produce an answer the instance didn't actually support? Say plainly, don't bury either.  
Second: identify the subject/genre/tradition/claim fully. Sort each major claim from Phases 1–4 as: (a) real information the instance's own content couldn't supply, (b) correctly seen but reframed by the identification, or (c) already fully correct, now just labeled.  
Third: state plainly which parts of this analysis you're confident reflect a checked process, and which are fluent narration of a check you can't verify happened. Don't smooth this over.

## Applications:

A few domains where that unbundling is the actual point:

**Journalistic/documentary photographs.** Press photos of protests, war, disaster, or political events arrive pre-loaded with framing vocabulary — "clash," "unrest," "liberation," "aggressor" — before anyone describes who is standing where, who has a weapon and who doesn't, who is restrained and who is restraining. E would force: bodies, contact points, who has instrumental control, who doesn't — then ask whether the caption's chosen verb is doing description or advocacy. This is close to how careful fact-checkers already work, but E operationalizes it as a repeatable protocol rather than trained editorial instinct.

**Institutional/corporate imagery — org charts, stock photos, annual report covers.** These are built entirely out of genre-shorthand: "leadership," "collaboration," "diversity," "innovation." A literal-register pass (who's positioned higher, who's centered, who's touching the table and who's standing behind it, whose hands are visible doing something versus whose are folded) can surface status hierarchies the polished vocabulary is specifically designed to soften.

**Medical/clinical images.** Same structure as the guru-seal case: euphemism-by-genre is extremely strong here (a scan described as "unremarkable," a wound described in clinical Latin) precisely where a plain physical description might carry more actionable weight for a patient. E's audit — "would someone unfamiliar with this genre reach for this word" — is a good check on whether a report is informing or softening.

**Historical propaganda and advertising.** Both genres are *built* to make the viewer's recognition do the persuasive work before conscious inspection — heroic poses, idealized proportions, staged intimacy. E is close to a reverse-engineering tool here: strip the genre-vocabulary that the image was designed to trigger, and see what's physically, mechanically actually depicted (who's elevated, who's diminished, who has agency in frame).

**Text, not just images.** D was already text-native (the riddle case), so E's fusion suggests a text version: legal or policy documents where a term of art ("collateral damage," "enhanced interrogation," "right-sizing") is doing exactly the register-laundering Phase 2 targets. The same confession-then-audit structure — name the euphemism you're trained to reach for, then describe the mechanical fact underneath it before allowing the term back in — is a usable rewrite for reading contracts, ToS documents, or press releases skeptically.

**A caution about domain fit, though.** All of your test cases had a genuinely legible physical/mechanical layer under the genre-vocabulary (grip, contact, posture, exposure). That's not guaranteed everywhere. For heavily abstract material — pure statistics, abstract art with no representational content, ideological arguments with no physical referent at all — there may be no "spade" under the euphemism to call anything; Phase 1's audit could just churn on vocabulary with nothing structural to recover, producing pseudo-literalism (very confident descriptions of "form" and "density" that don't actually catch a hidden asymmetry, because there wasn't a smuggled-in verdict to unmask). The method's power seems tied specifically to cases where **rhetoric is doing real interpretive labor that a mechanical description can independently verify or contradict** — asymmetric power, obscured agency, softened harm. Where that labor isn't happening, E is just an expensive way of restating the obvious slowly.





## Theory of prompt

**Representativeness heuristic (Tversky & Kahneman)**  
"Genre Ghost" is this heuristic given a name: matching an instance to a prototype and importing the prototype's properties without checking base rates or mechanism. Phase 3's line about resemblance not being evidence is close to a direct restatement of the finding.

**Bias blind spot / naive realism (Pronin, Lin, Ross)**  
Phase 0 forces a pre-declaration of motive before any content is examined — a direct countermeasure to the finding that people are far better at spotting bias in others' conclusions than in their own reasoning in the moment. Declaring the motive in advance doesn't eliminate the pull, but converts an invisible influence into a visible, named one to check against later.

**Shklovsky's defamiliarization (ostranenie) — Russian Formalism**  
Shklovsky's claim was that habituation makes us stop perceiving things and start merely recognizing them; art's job is to "make the stone stony" again by disrupting automatic perception. Phase 2's atomic-inventory move — describing a picture as "bodies, objects, marks, density, boundaries" rather than as its recognized subject — forces perception before recognition, stripping the automatized label to see the raw material underneath.

**Intelligence-analysis tradecraft, especially Richards Heuer's Analysis of Competing Hypotheses**  
Heuer's framework exists to fight analysts converging on the first plausible hypothesis and then reading incoming evidence as confirming it, rather than testing it against genuine alternatives. His method requires listing hypotheses and evidence before judging fit, and training analysts to notice when evidence is merely consistent with a story versus diagnostic of it. Phase 3's mechanism check is a diagnosticity test in exactly this sense: is this fact only compatible with the expected story, or does it actually discriminate for it.

**Bayesian updating cycle**  
The five-phase shape mirrors this more precisely than any single named heuristic. Phase 0 forces an explicit prior (the expected guess, plus what motive is quietly weighting it); Phase 2 is a likelihood pass — what the evidence says, examined without reference to the hypothesis it might support; Phase 5 is the posterior — the reconciled belief, sorted by what the evidence alone could and couldn't supply. Bayesian practice insists on writing down the prior before looking at data for the same reason: priors formed after seeing the data get contaminated by it, silently.

**Feynman's "first principle"**  
"You must not fool yourself, and you are the easiest person to fool" is a closer-register ancestor for Phases 2–3 than the more formal apparatus of falsifiability or mechanistic explanation — folk epistemology doing the same job with less machinery.

**Toulmin's model of argument**  
Phase 2's "checkable vs. merely asserted" is the data/warrant distinction: is there a stated ground for this claim, or just an implicit license to believe it.

**Goffman's frame analysis**  
Phase 1's "what does this convention reward a responder for giving" mirrors Goffman's point that a frame doesn't just describe a situation, it organizes participants' expected behavior within it.

**Motivated reasoning (Kunda)**  
Phase 0b's forced naming of the self-flattering conclusion operationalizes the central claim of this literature: directional goals bias search and evaluation before the reasoner notices.

**Calibration / overconfidence research**  
Phase 5's third clause — distinguishing a checked process from fluent narration of a check — is exactly what this literature studies: confidently reported introspection that doesn't reliably track actual process.

Taken together, the prompt sits at the intersection of three lineages: cognitive-bias debiasing technique (name the prior, name the motive, before evidence contaminates them), formalist defamiliarization (describe the raw material before the recognized genre), and intelligence tradecraft (test evidence for diagnostic value, not mere consistency) — closed by a calibration-research move that most debiasing frameworks skip: asking whether the checking itself was real or just fluently narrated.
