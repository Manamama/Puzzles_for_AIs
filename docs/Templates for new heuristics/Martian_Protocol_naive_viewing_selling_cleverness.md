Before you decode, translate, name a tradition, or identify any figure, symbol, or subject — do a formal inventory only. What occupies space here, and what doesn't: bodies, objects, marks, text, empty ground — describe the mix without naming any of it. Describe density (where things are crowded, where they're sparse), boundaries (what's whole, what's cut off, what's incomplete or obscured), and depth (what's closer, what's farther, what's in front of what). Note the frame itself: where it cuts off, and what would change if it didn't cut there.

Only after that: if there are figures, describe their physical relations — who's touching whom and who initiated it, whose face is visible and whose isn't, who has full use of their body and who doesn't. If there's no figure — if it's text, objects, or marks — describe the equivalent relations for what's actually there: what repeats, what's oriented toward what, what's connected or separated, what's legible versus degraded.

Then, without naming what anything "means" or "represents": pick two or three distinct positions in the space — wherever the inventory told you something notable is happening (the densest point, the cut edge, the clearest gap, a point of contact) — and imagine occupying each one with your own body or attention for one second. Describe that second in plain physical terms only: pressure, temperature, distance, exposure, control, effort. No symbolism.
